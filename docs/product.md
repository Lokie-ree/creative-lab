# Standards Alignment Framework

Mapping discovery-first pedagogy to Louisiana Student Standards for Mathematics (LSSM).

---

## Purpose

This document demonstrates how the pedagogical principles in [philosophy.md](./philosophy.md) satisfy LSSM rigor requirements and move students across Achievement Level Descriptors (ALDs). It serves as the product specification for module design.

---

## Core Pedagogical Principles

Four design principles (defined in [philosophy.md](./philosophy.md)) drive all module interactions:

- **Discovery before formula** — Students engage with mathematical phenomena before being handed the formal shorthand.
- **Manipulation before explanation** — Learning begins with tactile or visual movement of mathematical objects, creating a mental model before verbal or written definitions.
- **The "earned reveal"** — Access to formal notation and symbolic representation is granted only after a student has established conceptual grounding.
- **Visual confirmation over multiple choice** — Feedback comes from the success of a visual construction or manipulation, not from selecting a static answer.

These principles map directly to the three components of rigor defined by the Louisiana Department of Education: **Conceptual Understanding**, **Procedural Skill and Fluency**, and **Application**.

---

## LSSM Rigor Components

The LSSM "Guide to Rigor" requires mathematics instruction to balance three components. Discovery-first design naturally satisfies all three:

| Interactive principle   | LSSM alignment & justification |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **The earned reveal**   | **Conceptual understanding:** Ensures students grasp the "why" of a mathematical relationship before the "how" of its notation, preventing symbolic manipulation without meaning. |
| **Manipulation-first**  | **Procedural skill and fluency:** Builds an intuitive sense of operations (e.g., factoring, shifting graphs) before formalizing steps, so fluency is rooted in visual logic. |
| **Visual confirmation** | **Application:** Lets students validate their models against visual outcomes, mirroring real-world cycles of formulation and validation. |

### Example: Visual Confirmation and A-REI.A.1

Standard A1: A-REI.A.1 requires students to *"Construct a viable argument to justify a solution method."* In a discovery-first module, the student's manipulation serves as the primary evidence for their justification. When a student drags a ghost wave to match a target and the system confirms the match, they have demonstrated understanding through construction—not through selecting a pre-written explanation.

## Algebra I Standards Alignment

Algebra I standards focus on interpreting structure in expressions and functions. Discovery-first design makes abstract structure visible and manipulable.

### Standard Cluster Examples

| Standard | Discovery-First Interaction | ALD Level Reached |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **A1: A-SSE.A.1b** — Interpret complicated expressions by viewing parts as single entities | Students manipulate *(1+r)^n* as a single factor in compound interest before seeing the expanded form | Level 4 (Mastery) |
| **A1: F-IF.B.4** — Interpret key features of functions | Students drag parameters to observe how intercepts and end behavior change, then formalize the relationship | Level 4 (Mastery) |
| **A1: F-IF.B.6** — Calculate and interpret rate of change | Interactive graphing lets students compare rates across intervals visually before computing symbolically | Level 5 (Advanced) |

## Geometry Standards Alignment

Geometry standards use transformations to define congruence and similarity. Discovery-first modules make transformations visible as functions that map points.

### Standard Cluster Examples

| Standard | Discovery-First Interaction | ALD Level Reached |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **GM: G-CO.A.2** — Describe transformations as functions | Students drag a ghost shape to predict transformation output, then see the reveal animation follow the transformation path | Level 4 (Mastery) |
| **GM: G-SRT.C.6** — Use similarity to define trigonometric ratios | Students observe that side ratios in similar right triangles remain constant, discovering sine and cosine as angle properties | Level 5 (Advanced) |
| **8.G.A.1–3** — Understand congruence through rigid motions | Students predict image positions for translations, reflections, rotations, then build 2-step sequences in capstone | Level 5 (Advanced) |

## Achievement Level Descriptors (ALDs)

ALDs define student proficiency across five levels. Discovery-first modules are designed to move students from Level 3 (Basic) to Level 5 (Advanced) by requiring demonstration of understanding rather than recognition.

### ALD Progression Through Discovery-First Design

| Level | Performance Characteristics | Module Design Strategy |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| **Level 3 (Basic)** | Identifying components, routine calculation, recognizing patterns | Entry point: Students observe and manipulate without formal notation |
| **Level 4 (Mastery)** | Describing transformations with coordinates, determining congruence through transformations | Coordinate reveal: Formalization appears after spatial understanding is demonstrated |
| **Level 5 (Advanced)** | Describing sequences of transformations to justify congruence, analyzing equivalent expressions | Capstone tasks: Students build multi-step sequences or analyze structure they've already manipulated |

### Example: Rigid Motions Module ALD Progression

1. **Level 3 Entry** — Students predict transformation outputs spatially (no coordinates visible)
2. **Level 4 Target** — Coordinate rules revealed after mastery; students connect spatial reasoning to algebraic representation
3. **Level 5 Capstone** — Students identify and describe 2-step transformation sequences, justifying congruence

This progression is intentional: understanding precedes notation, and notation serves as confirmation rather than prerequisite.

## Module Design Requirements

Every module must satisfy these requirements to ensure standards alignment:

### 1. Standards Grounding
- Explicitly map to LSSM content standards (e.g., 8.G.A.1–3, A1: F-IF.B.4)
- Identify target ALD levels (entry, primary target, capstone)
- Document which rigor components are addressed (Conceptual, Procedural, Application)

### 2. Discovery-First Interaction
- Students manipulate before seeing formal notation
- Visual confirmation provides immediate feedback
- Earned reveals appear only after demonstrated understanding

### 3. ALD Progression Evidence
- Design interactions that produce evidence of ALD-level performance
- Level 3: Observation and basic manipulation
- Level 4: Connecting representations (spatial ↔ algebraic)
- Level 5: Multi-step reasoning and justification

### 4. Rigor Balance
- **Conceptual Understanding:** Manipulation reveals "why" before "how"
- **Procedural Fluency:** Notation appears as efficient shorthand for understood concepts
- **Application:** Real-world contexts and modeling cycles where appropriate

---

## Summary

Discovery-first pedagogy naturally satisfies LSSM rigor requirements because it requires students to demonstrate understanding through construction rather than recognition. By designing interactions that move students from Level 3 (Basic) to Level 5 (Advanced), modules become primary drivers of achievement rather than supplementary practice tools.
